# Grand Master Rare check

This site is a **week-1 sell-through model**, not a serialized-card census. On that job it is internally coherent. As a check against how many Grand Master Rares have already been pulled, listed, and sold, it is incomplete — and this page says so on purpose.

## Snapshot the box math uses

Date on the dashboard: **2026-09-11** (street **Sep 4**, day 7).

| Number | Value | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Proven sold | **94,290+** | Target first-party 62k + TCGPlayer sealed 28,290 + Amazon marketplace 4,000 |
| Estimated sold | **191,400** | Proven, then stretched (mass ×1.20, TCGPlayer treated as 25% of remaining hobby) |
| Estimated print | **220,110** | Estimated sold + ~15% still in channel. US slice ~193.7k |

Walmart and GameStop stay blank on purpose. Main dashboard number is estimated **sold**, not print.

## The GMR math this site is not doing yet

Konami locked Americas supply at **18 names × 100 serials = 1,800** Grand Master Rares. Europe-distributed markets get another 1,800 with an `E` suffix. That 1,800 is not an estimate.

If the tracker’s ~220k Americas print is in the right zip code:

**About 1 Grand Master Rare per 122 tuck boxes**  
(220,110 ÷ 1,800 ≈ 122)

That is **not** the “1 in 881 boxes” figure floating around articles. 1-in-881 only works if print is ~1.6 million boxes — which is incompatible with Target moving 62k+ units at $34.99 in week 1 and TCGPlayer still sitting around $40–43 vs $34.99 retail.

How the cross-check is supposed to work (when we have openings):

> Implied print = 1,800 ÷ (Grand Master Rares found ÷ boxes opened).  
> If that disagrees with sell-through by more than 2×, say which input is weak — **don’t average them**.

Until openings are logged, the GMR cell stays empty.

## What the public GMR record actually shows

Confirmed, named activity in the first week-plus is real but still a **thin sample**, not a pop report:

- Early eBay: five-figure sales (press cited Dark Magical Curtain around **$25k**; Kuriboh / Flame Wingman near **$15k**).
- Regional heatmaps with US pins plus UK / AU / EU hits by day 2 (Europe `E` serials are a separate 1,800).
- Other public pulls: dad’s Kuriboh `/100`, a shop customer hitting Cyberse Contract Witch **#48/100**, claims of Winged Kuriboh in a 3rd box.
- Big opening samples that **didn’t** hit are also data: large filmed samples with **0** GMR; an EU multi-case log with **0** GMR.

That pattern — a handful of loud five-figure listings **plus** a lot of dead air in case openings — is what **1-in-122** looks like when only a sliver of opened boxes get posted.

Worked example: if **15,000** boxes were ripped by streamers, shops, and casual rippers in week 1, expected Americas GMRs pulled ≈ 15,000 × 1,800 ÷ 220,110 ≈ **123**. Publicly listed or tweeted might be 20–40. The rest sit in binders, go to graders, sell in Discords, or get pulled by Target customers who never post serials.

So “several already sold and more already mapped” does **not** by itself blow up 220k. It *would* blow it up if a large, counted opening book (say 5,000+ filmed boxes) produced a hit rate 2× off 1-in-122. That book does not exist yet.

## Where the box math is firmer vs softer

**Stronger**

- Separates proven / estimated sold / print instead of one magic number.
- Refuses to invent Walmart / GameStop solds.
- Does not stack eBay / Whatnot on top of TCGPlayer.
- Treats Amazon marketplace as mostly incremental at $35 retail / ~$42 market (flipping after fees is usually a loser) — reasonable for week 1.
- Shows bands on the soft knobs.

**Softer — where a real GMR census would bite**

1. **Target 62,000** does a lot of work. If that badge is junk (not true week-1 solds), the whole pyramid shifts.
2. **TCGPlayer treated as 25% of remaining hobby** is the biggest soft stretch. If TCGPlayer is 40% of hobby, estimated sold drops hard.
3. **Display = 10 boxes** is US case math. Wrong conversion contaminates the TCGPlayer total.
4. **~15% still unsold after 7 days** is a first-month prior, not a measurement. Target sold out argues mass is gone; hobby cases may still sit.
5. TCGPlayer 3-month sold mixes **presale + street** (flagged; still used).
6. No GMR opening log means the one hard Konami-fixed supply number (**1,800**) is unused so far.

## Verdict

The box math can be true at the same time the “lottery ticket / 1-in-881” story is false. A ~220k Americas print with 1,800 serials is a **mass-retail product with a scarce chase seeded through it**, which matches:

- Target moving tens of thousands at sticker
- boxes still near retail on day 7
- multiple GMRs already public and trading five figures
- most filmed case openings still showing zero

Listed / sold serials do **not** yet make 220k “not make sense.” There are not enough *counted openings with counted hits* to invert that print. Public listings fit **1-in-~120** plus a very high list-price filter (only the loudest names hit headlines).

**What would make the site stop making sense**

- a real opening ledger showing ~1 GMR per 40–50 boxes (print closer to 70–90k), or
- ~1 GMR per 400+ boxes (print closer to 700k+), or
- Target 62k turning out to be junk data.

Until someone logs serials against boxes opened — the missing check — treat:

- **94k proven** as the only hard floor  
- **191k sold / 220k print** as a plausible but knob-heavy week-1 estimate  
- the GMR market as confirming scarcity of *named serials*, not confirming or killing the print run
